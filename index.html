P1 — Community Introduction
About Gopal Goshala

Gopal Goshala in Rayapur, Hubballi is a shelter that cares for cows with the help of caretakers, volunteers, students, donors, and management members. Daily activities such as feeding, cleaning, and providing medical care are supported through donations and volunteer efforts.

The goshala community believes in seva (service), and many people from nearby colleges and local areas come forward to help. Volunteers regularly participate in different activities and contribute their time for the welfare of the cows.

Even with limited manpower, the caretakers work hard to manage daily operations. The main challenge is not the lack of volunteers, but the difficulty in coordinating their efforts effectively. A simple system to connect volunteers with the tasks that need to be completed can help improve the overall functioning of the goshala.

Understanding Community

Community Overview

Gopal Goshala is run through collective community effort. Caretakers maintain daily routines while volunteers from nearby colleges and localities offer their time and energy. Donors provide the financial and material support that keeps operations going.

The spirit of shared service runs deep here. People come not out of obligation but out of genuine care for the cows. The management oversees planning and decision-making, working closely with caretakers to ensure nothing falls through the cracks.

The core challenge the goshala faces is not one of motivation or resources — it is one of coordination. Matching willing volunteers to the right tasks at the right time is where the gap lies.

Core Problem

What We Found

Most care giving activities in the goshala, such as moving cows, feeding, and cleaning, are carried out during morning and evening routine hours. These time-intensive activities are mainly handled by only a small number of caretakers. The smooth functioning of the goshala depends on maintaining these repetitive routines consistently every day.

W1
Because volunteers show up without knowing what tasks need to be done — there is no structured system to communicate daily needs.
W2
Caretakers focus on physical tasks and have no bandwidth to onboard or direct volunteers during busy hours.
W3
The goshala has grown in volunteer footfall but not in organisational tools to match that scale.
W4
Coordination happens informally — word of mouth, phone calls — which breaks down under pressure.
W5
Because there is no lightweight mechanism that fits into the existing rhythm of daily routines — a simple matching system between tasks and available volunteers is missing.

Volunteer Pre-Registration System
We are not making a new coping mechanism — we are improvising an existing coping mechanism of the goshala.
Volunteers register in advance for specific tasks and time slots
Caretakers know exactly who is coming and for what
Works within existing daily routines without disruption
No digital literacy required — accessible to everyone
Both caretakers and volunteers benefit directly
Volunteers kept directly in the design process from the start
Context Analysis

PESTLE Analysis

Framework Overview
We mapped the broader environment around Gopal Goshala to understand the forces that shape volunteer coordination and community participation.
Economic
Goshala runs on donations; paid coordination staff is not feasible. The solution must be low-cost and sustainable on community goodwill alone.
Social
Strong local culture of service. Students and residents are willing to participate — the gap is in structured engagement, not motivation.
Technological
Smartphone penetration is moderate. Older caretakers are more comfortable with physical boards than apps; the solution bridges both worlds.
Environmental
Cow welfare directly tied to consistent daily routines. Gaps in care have immediate, visible impact on animal health and goshala cleanliness.
Testing

Our Pilot Plan

Phase 01

Map the Routines

Document morning and evening tasks in detail with caretaker input.

Phase 03

Deploy the System

Launch the pre-registration system at the goshala entry; train caretakers on reviewing sign-ups daily.

Phase 02

Co-Design with Volunteers

Run a workshop with frequent volunteers to shape the registration format together.

Phase 04

Observe & Iterate

Track volunteer participation over 2 weeks and refine based on feedback from both caretakers and volunteers.

Learning & Iteration

Feedback & Refinements

What the Community Said
Caretakers appreciated the clarity that pre-registration would bring. Volunteers felt more confident about arriving knowing tasks would be pre-assigned. Management saw it as easy to maintain without extra overhead.
Key Iterations
  • 1Added time-slot selection so volunteers choose morning or evening shift during registration
  • 2Simplified task names to be instantly understandable without explanation
  • 3Added a caretaker confirmation step to close the feedback loop

What We Learned

Our Reflections

🔍
On Observation

Spending time at the goshala without an agenda revealed insights no survey could have captured. The real problem appeared only when we watched, not when we asked.

🤝
On Collaboration

We kept volunteers directly in the process — helping both caretakers and volunteers shape the solution together. This ensured tools fit naturally into how people already work and contribute.

🌱
On Simplicity

The best solutions don't add complexity — they remove friction. A simple registration system beats a full app when the community values ease and trust over features.
